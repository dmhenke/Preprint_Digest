1. Genome-wide association and multi-omics functional screens reveal the genetic architecture of foveal development

MEDRXIV · DOI: 10.64898/2026.06.11.26355452 · Score: ███████░░░ 0.787

Core Problem: Foveal hypoplasia causes visual impairment across congenital eye disorders, yet the genetic programmes governing foveal development remain poorly characterised and no tractable model exists for foveal disease

Proposed Solution: In the first genome-wide association study of foveal hypoplasia, we identified 42 sentinel variants mapping to 54 effector genes supported by >= 2 criteria from a variant-to-gene framework incorporating developmental multi-omics. Disruption of six effector genes using mutant lines and CRISPR knockouts in the zebrafish high acuity zone recapitulates structural, functional, and ultrastructural hallmarks of foveal hypoplasia, establishing the first vertebrate disease model

Key Impact: These findings should inform mechanistic studies of macular disease

Methodology: Crispr techniques were central to the study design.

3. MAHLER: Integrating Metadynamics and Inverse Folding to Predict Antibody-Antigen Kinetics

BIORXIV · DOI: 10.64898/2026.06.10.731383 · Score: ███████░░░ 0.708

Core Problem: Binding kinetics are crucial for antibody function, shaping pharmacokinetics and in vivo efficacy beyond what equilibrium affinity captures

Proposed Solution: We present "Metadynamics-Anchored Hybrid Learning for Engineering off-Rates (MAHLER)", a fully open-source machine learning/physics hybrid method that predicts relative antibody-antigen residence times at scale. Incorporating inverse-folding models into molecular dynamics simulations, MAHLER shows first-in-class screening-grade accuracy in calculating relative antibody-antigen dissociation kinetics across a family of point mutants

Key Impact: This provides practical kinetics-aware complement to current computational design approaches that focus primarily on binding affinity for antibody-antigen complexes

Methodology: Machine Learning techniques were central to the study design.

4. A Graph-based QSAR Modeling Pipeline for Predicting In vitro PubChem Assays and In vivo Human Hepatotoxicity: Mechanistic Analysis of Caspase-3/7 Activation

BIORXIV · DOI: 10.64898/2026.06.10.731399 · Score: ██████░░░░ 0.679

Core Problem: Accurately and rapidly predicting the complex mechanisms of chemical toxicity—specifically those involving both mitochondrial membrane potential collapse and Caspase-3/7 activation—remains a significant challenge for environmental risk assessment.

Proposed Solution: The authors developed a comprehensive Quantitative Structure-Activity Relationship (QSAR) pipeline that integrates advanced machine learning models, including Graph Transformers (GTs) and Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), to predict both Caspase activity and mitochondrial membrane potential disruption using molecular graph representations.

Key Impact: This framework significantly advances predictive toxicology by providing a robust tool capable of assessing multiple, interconnected endpoints—from basic cellular assays to complex human liver injury (DILI)—using structural information alone.

Methodology: The pipeline leverages the inherent topological information of molecules via molecular graphs and employs consensus ensembles of advanced graph models (GTs/GNNs) to enhance predictive accuracy across diverse data imbalance scenarios.

5. PeptiDIA: A Machine Learning Framework for Enhanced Peptide Identification in Fast-Gradient Data-Independent Acquisition Proteomics

BIORXIV · DOI: 10.64898/2026.06.10.731224 · Score: ██████░░░░ 0.672

Core Problem: Data-independent acquisition (DIA) mass spectrometry has become increasingly prevalent in proteomics as advances in instrumentation, chromatography, and computational analysis have enabled robust proteome identification across complex biological samples

Proposed Solution: However, analytical depth achieved with fast chromatographic gradients remains lower than that obtained using long-gradients, reflecting a throughput-depth trade-off. Here, we present PeptiDIA, a machine learning framework that enhances peptide identification in fast-gradient DIA data by leveraging paired fast and long-gradient acquisitions from identical samples
