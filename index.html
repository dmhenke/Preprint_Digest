Core Problem: Predicting the efficacy of chemically modified small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) is challenging due to limited public data and the inability of current machine learning models to effectively model chemical diversity.

Proposed Solution: The authors developed FENNEC, a novel deep learning framework that combines temporal convolutional networks with thermodynamic descriptors and large-scale embeddings derived from RNA foundation models to predict siRNA activity across diverse chemical modifications.

Key Impact: This tool enables rational, chemically aware design of therapeutic siRNAs, accelerating the preclinical development process by predicting performance for novel molecular structures.

Methodology: FENNEC integrates language-model-derived embeddings alongside classical descriptors to capture both local chemical determinants and broader target-context information in a unified predictive architecture.

3. Cellfm-datasets: A Unified Data Infrastructure for Single-Cell and Spatial Transcriptomics Foundation Model Pretraining

BIORXIV · DOI: 10.64898/2026.06.11.731508 · Score: ██████░░░░ 0.690

Core Problem: Large-scale cell foundation models are increasingly limited not only by model architecture, but also by the data infrastructure required to repeatedly sample sparse transcriptomic profiles from out-of-core cohorts

Proposed Solution: AnnData/H5AD has become a standard exchange format for single-cell and spatial omics analysis, yet its HDF5-backed layout is not designed for high-frequency random mini-batch loading under multi-worker and distributed pretraining. We present Cellfm-datasets, a data infrastructure artifact that converts H5AD cohorts into a self-describing compressed sparse row (CSR) memmap layout and exposes the resulting corpus through Hugging Face Dataset and IterableDataset interfaces

Key Impact: By moving sparse single-cell and spatial corpora from model-specific loader code into reusable, validated, and framework-native dataset artifacts, this design may reduce the engineering burden of reproducible cell foundation model pretraining and make repeated training runs, model comparisons, and mixed-modality data reuse easier to standardize

Methodology: Single-Cell techniques were central to the study design.

Core Problem: Spatial multi-omics holds great promise for dissecting complex biological processes, though inherent technical constraints continue to limit its widespread adoption

Proposed Solution: Currently, most studies therefore measure distinct omics features on separate tissue sections, necessitating spatial diagonal integration. Key Impact: Overall, our approach bridges readily accessible single-omics measurements across weakly anchored tissue sections, enabling unified spatial multi-omics characterization and system-level tissue analysis
