1. CryoARC: Atomic-resolution conformational landscapes of protein assemblies from cryo-EM single particles with evolutionary priors

BIORXIV · DOI: 10.64898/2026.05.25.727696 · Score: ░░░░░░░░░░ 0.000

Core Problem: Single-particle cryo-electron microscopy (cryo-EM) reveals structural heterogeneity in macromolecular complexes, but recovering continuous conformational landscapes at high resolution remains challenging

Proposed Solution: Here, we introduce CryoARC, a deep learning framework that integrates evolutionary sequence representations with cryo-EM particle images to reconstruct continuous conformational ensembles at atomic resolution. CryoARC combines a latent representation of particle heterogeneity with a sequence-conditioned structure decoder inspired by protein structure prediction architectures, enabling direct prediction of particle-specific atomic structures

Key Impact: fr/GruLab/CryoARC

Methodology: Deep Learning techniques were central to the study design.

3. Systematic prediction and functional analysis of amino acid residues determining product specificity in the plant oxidosqualene cyclase superfamily

BIORXIV · DOI: 10.64898/2026.05.26.727834 · Score: ░░░░░░░░░░ 0.000

Core Problem: ** Predicting the specific product profile of oxidosqualene cyclases (OSCs) based solely on amino acid sequence remains a significant challenge in biochemistry. **

Proposed Solution: ** The authors developed a structure-based computational framework that integrates differential conservation, physico-chemical properties, and binding pocket electrostatics to identify amino acid determinants governing OSC product specificity. **

Key Impact: ** This work provides a detailed blueprint for engineering OSCs, enabling the precise, stepwise reprogramming of natural triterpene scaffolds for potential biotechnological applications. **

Methodology: ** The study utilized molecular dynamics simulations to mechanistically support how subtle structural changes in the active site, specifically altering volume, water access, and proton-elimination chemistry, dictate product specificity.

4. Diploidy alters the path of fluconazole adaptation in C. glabrata

BIORXIV · DOI: 10.64898/2026.05.22.727327 · Score: ░░░░░░░░░░ 0.000

Core Problem: The evolutionary pathways and genetic mechanisms by which *C. glabrata* adapts to fluconazole differ significantly between haploid and diploid states.

Proposed Solution: The authors performed experimental evolution on both haploid and diploid strains of *C. glabrata* under fluconazole stress, tracking fitness changes and genetic mutations over many generations.

Key Impact: This work reveals that the ploidy state dictates the adaptive strategy, demonstrating that diploids utilize distinct, complex genetic mechanisms (e.g., aneuploidy and specific gene combinations) compared to haploids to achieve high-level drug resistance.

Methodology: The study utilized competitive fitness assays and tracked the emergence of specific aneuploidies (e.g., trisomies of chrF, chrG, chrI, and chrC) in response to escalating fluconazole concentrations.

Core Problem: Recent advances in neuroscience have highlighted the central role of glial cells, particularly astrocytes, in regulating neural network activity through calcium-dependent neuron-glia communication

Proposed Solution: In parallel, nanostructured cluster-assembled materials have emerged as promising candidates for developing brain-machine interfaces, because of their biomimetic morphology, mechanotransductive properties and neuromorphic behavior.
