2. Cellpin enables reference-based imputation and denoising of spatial transcriptomes

BIORXIV · DOI: 10.64898/2026.06.02.729566 · Score: ███████░░░ 0.719

Core Problem: Spatially resolved transcriptomics enables gene expression profiling within tissue architecture, but targeted panels leave much of the transcriptome unmeasured and spatial artifacts such as RNA diffusion and segmentation errors introduce technical noise

Proposed Solution: These limitations necessitate computational imputation and denoising, yet existing methods typically incorporate spatial measurements during training, limiting scalability and risking the embedding of technology-specific artifacts into learned representations. To address this, we present cellpin, a variational autoencoder trained exclusively on single-cell RNA sequencing data, using teacher-student latent distillation and noise-simulating augmentations to jointly impute unmeasured genes and denoise spatial profiles without requiring cross-modality alignment

Key Impact: In full-transcriptome Atera data, cellpin reduces residual spatial noise and improves cell-state resolution, providing a scalable and principled foundation for biological discovery from spatial transcriptomics data

Methodology: Single-Cell techniques were central to the study design.

3. Reconstructing clone-resolved transcriptional programs from bulk tumor sequencing

BIORXIV · DOI: 10.64898/2026.01.15.699695 · Score: ███████░░░ 0.701

Core Problem: Tumor clones acquire distinct transcriptional programs as they evolve, but bulk RNA-seq averages over clonal mixtures and obscures the lineage-specific biology that DNA-sequencing reveals

Proposed Solution: We present PICTographPlus, the first method to infer clone-resolved transcriptional programs by integrating bulk DNA-derived clonal phylogenies and proportions with bulk RNA-seq alone, without single-cell data. Benchmarked against experimentally measured ground truth, scDNA/scRNA co-profiled cells from a wellDR-seq cancer dataset, across 320 pseudo-bulk replicates spanning four tumor purities and four sample counts and evaluated under seven regularization models, PICTographPlus recovers clone-level expression at mean Pearson r [&ge;] 0

Key Impact: PICTographPlus turns standard bulk assays into clone-resolved transcriptional maps, enabling retrospective analyses where single-cell profiling is impractical

Methodology: Single-Cell techniques were central to the study design.

4. Extrachromosomal DNA as a Causal Instrument for Spatial Multi-Omics

BIORXIV · DOI: 10.64898/2026.06.02.729483 · Score: ██████░░░░ 0.669

Core Problem: Spatial transcriptomics, multiplex imaging, and computational pathology now map tissue organization at cellular resolution, but the analyses applied to these data remain correlational

Proposed Solution: Clustering and co-occurrence statistics describe which features appear together; they cannot say which feature drives the others. We propose a framework for causal inference in spatial multi-omics built on a specific feature of extrachromosomal DNA (ecDNA)

Key Impact: The framework recovers known oncogene-to-phenotype effects from simulated data, and we outline its application to real tumor sections, including multi-instrument settings where independent ecDNA species carrying different oncogenes enable factorial designs within a single tumor

Methodology: Spatial Transcriptomics techniques were central to the study design.

5. CarotidMamba: Foundation Model-Enabled CTA Phenotyping of Symptomatic Carotid Plaques in a Multi-Center Retrospective Study

MEDRXIV · DOI: 10.64898/2026.06.02.26354776 · Score: ██████░░░░ 0.654

Core Problem: Current clinical decisions regarding carotid atherosclerotic disease rely primarily on measurable luminal stenosis, despite plaque vulnerability and symptomatic status being better predictors of short-term cerebrovascular risk.

Proposed Solution: The authors developed CarotidMamba, a deep learning framework, to automatically phenotype symptomatic carotid plaques using CTA images, thereby moving assessment beyond simple degree of stenosis.

Key Impact: This validated tool provides an interpretable, non-invasive method for assessing plaque vulnerability in symptomatic patients, improving the risk stratification of cerebral vascular disease.

Methodology: The framework utilizes a novel combination of dual foundation-model encoders with Mamba-based sequence modeling to achieve superior performance and calibration compared to standard CNN or transformer architectures.
