1. Proteomics-constrained deconvolution reveals spatial cell-type programs in tumours

BIORXIV · DOI: 10.64898/2026.06.01.729268 · Score: ████████░░ 0.806

Core Problem: Accurately resolving cell-type mixtures in spatial transcriptomics remains challenging, particularly in heterogeneous tumours where cell populations are intermixed and matched single-cell references may be unavailable or poorly aligned

Proposed Solution: Current deconvolution approaches either require high-quality scRNA-seq references, suffer from scalability limitations, or lack interpretability. We introduce PISTACHIO, a proteomics-informed spatial transcriptomics deconvolution framework based on constrained non-negative matrix factorization with a negative-binomial likelihood

Key Impact: Our approach remains robust under cell-type assignment errors, maintaining high correlation with ground-truth under moderate noise, and achieves fast runtime on standard hardware, enabling practical large-scale deployment

Methodology: Single-Cell techniques were central to the study design.

2. Vibe Coding Specificity Foundation Models

BIORXIV · DOI: 10.64898/2026.06.04.730134 · Score: ███████░░░ 0.743

Core Problem: Current methods for predicting molecular binding specificity rely on experimental screening or computational tools that lack general applicability across diverse biological recognition modalities.

Proposed Solution: The authors developed a Specificity Foundation Model (SFM), a physics-derived neural network architecture, which uses dual sequence encoders and symmetric contrastive learning to predict binding compatibility scores between any agent and target pair using only their sequences.

Key Impact: SFMs establish a powerful, unified computational framework that significantly augments the predictive power of molecular recognition across diverse fields, including immunology, gene regulation, and drug discovery.

Methodology: The model leverages the mathematical identity between Transformer softmax attention and the Boltzmann distribution governing molecular binding to create a sequence-native architecture applicable without needing structural information.

Core Problem: Cryogenic electron microscopy (cryo-EM) has become an increasingly important for structure-based drug discovery by enabling characterization of interactions between macromolecules and small-molecule ligands

Proposed Solution: However, computational interpretation of ligand density remains challenging, particularly when ligand locations are unknown or local map resolution is limited. Existing methods generally require well-resolved macromolecular structures and predefined binding sites, limiting their applicability during early-stage structure determination

4. Long-read RNA-seq resolves isoform-level and context-specific regulatory architecture of complex traits in cattle

BIORXIV · DOI: 10.64898/2026.06.04.730050 · Score: ███████░░░ 0.713

Core Problem: Traditional short-read RNA-seq lacks the resolution to fully capture full-length isoforms and complex alternative splicing, leaving a "missing regulation" gap in existing molecular atlases

Proposed Solution: Here, we present a population-scale, multi-omics dissection of bovine complex traits using matched deep whole-genome sequencing (30), long-read (ONT) and short-read RNA-seq, as well as metabolome data from 432 dairy cows across four distinct lactation stages. We expanded the bovine transcript atlas with 13,177 novel isoforms and fine-mapped 1,088,900 regulatory effects across 11 diverse molecular phenotypes

5. Scalable in vivo cardiac functional genomics with compressed AAV-Perturb-seq reveals a common mitochondrial response to perturbation

BIORXIV · DOI: 10.64898/2026.06.01.729445 · Score: ███████░░░ 0.709

Core Problem: Efficient identification of new targets to treat human disease requires a scalable way to link genotype to phenotype directly in the target organ

Proposed Solution: Pooled CRISPR screening with single-cell RNA sequencing as a readout (Perturb-seq) has emerged as a method for functional genomics but is typically applied in vitro and is limited in scale. Here, we combine in vivo Perturb-seq via adeno-associated virus (AAV)-mediated delivery with a statistical framework allowing for signal deconvolution after multiple random perturbations per cell (compressed Perturb-seq), to develop in vivo compressed AAV-Perturb-seq, a scalable way to perform high-throughput, cell-autonomous functional genomics in a desired target organ

Key Impact: Broadly, our work establishes a platform to facilitate in vivo functional genomics in a target organ with direct applicability to identifying therapeutic targets for the treatment of human disease

Methodology: Crispr techniques were central to the study design.
