2. Cell-specific regulatory circuits connect genetic variation to disease susceptibility

BIORXIV · DOI: 10.64898/2026.06.01.729215 · Score: ███████░░░ 0.705

Core Problem: Despite identifying thousands of genetic variants associated with immune diseases, most fall in non-coding regions, making the underlying regulatory mechanisms difficult to interpret.

Proposed Solution: The authors generated single-cell multi-omics data integrating transcriptomic and chromatin accessibility information from peripheral blood mononuclear cells stimulated by *C. albicans* to map disease-relevant regulatory elements. They then applied network analysis (SCENIC+) and identified dual-acting QTLs that overlap both eQTLs and caQTLs.

Key Impact: By identifying genetic variants that simultaneously affect chromatin accessibility and gene expression, this approach provides a robust framework for prioritizing causal regulatory targets implicated in immune disease risk.

Methodology: The study leveraged SCENIC+ to identify regulatory triplets—linking transcription factors, their predicted binding sites, and target genes—and assessed which of these were under genetic control.

BIORXIV · DOI: 10.64898/2026.06.02.729712 · Score: ██████░░░░ 0.692

Core Problem: Enzymes are essential biocatalysts across diverse industries, driving demand for high-performing variants

Proposed Solution: Foundation models are attractive for guiding enzyme discovery, but often lack the resolution to model subtle variations driving function within homologous families. Navigating these rugged functional landscapes to identify elite variants remains challenging and experimentally costly, even when guided by such models

Key Impact: Together, these results highlight that machine learning can provide mechanistic insight, and that integrating targeted data acquisition with iterative machine learning provides a more data-efficient discovery strategy than relying on generic model scale

5. ORIGAMI: Orientation-Aware Graph Neural Network for Assessing Multimeric Interfaces of Protein Complex Structures

BIORXIV · DOI: 10.64898/2026.05.31.729128 · Score: ██████░░░░ 0.676

Core Problem: Deep learning-based protein structure prediction methods have led to a paradigm-shift in computational structural biology, yet reliably assessing the quality of computationally predicted multimeric structures remains challenging

Proposed Solution: Recent methods have demonstrated benefits of employing graph neural networks for assessing multimeric interfaces of protein complexes, but ignore geometric orientational features naturally occurring in 3-dimensional protein conformational space and act only on scalar weights. We present ORIGAMI, an orientation-aware graph neural network for assessing multimeric interfaces of protein complex structures that leverages both scalar and 3D vector node representations to perform symmetry-aware geometric operations while maintaining SO(3)-equivariance by capturing fine-grained orientational relationships between residues across protein-protein interfaces to estimate the interface Local Distance Difference Test (iLDDT) score

Key Impact: com/Bhattacharya-Lab/ORIGAMI

Methodology: Deep Learning techniques were central to the study design.
