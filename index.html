1. GPU accelerated population genetics statistics using pg_gpu

BIORXIV · DOI: 10.64898/2026.05.29.728868 · Score: ██████░░░░ 0.672

Core Problem: Population genetics summary statistics-- diversity, divergence, linkage disequilibrium, selection scans, and dimensionality reduction-- are fundamental across human, agricultural, and ecological genomics

Proposed Solution: As whole-genome sequencing datasets have grown to hundreds of thousands of individuals, the cost of computing these statistics on conventional CPU implementations has become a major bottleneck: windowed scans of a single chromosome arm can take hours to days, and computation of pairwise linkage-disequilibrium statistics useful for demographic inference scales as O(n^2) in sample size, often exceeding wall-clock budgets entirely. We present pg_gpu, a Python library implementing a comprehensive catalog of population-genetics summary statistics as fused CUDA kernels on NVIDIA GPUs

Key Impact: Whole chromosome arm scans, lostruct screens, and calculation of LD statistics complete on a single NVIDIA A100 in seconds to a few minutes

Methodology: Standard experimental and analytical methods techniques were central to the study design.

2. DanioDecima: A DNA sequence-to-function model of zebrafish embryogenesis

BIORXIV · DOI: 10.64898/2026.05.29.728876 · Score: ██████░░░░ 0.636

Core Problem: Deep learning DNA sequence-to-function models offer the promise of gaining mechanistic insights into genome regulation, however their performance is often limited by data scarcity in the species of interest

Proposed Solution: We present DanioDecima, a zebrafish-specific model leveraging transfer learning from human and mouse-trained models to predict tissue- and cell-type-specific gene expression during zebrafish embryogenesis. Initializing DanioDecima with pretrained human and mouse Borzoi and Decima weights raises the median pseudobulk Pearson r substantially across cell-types and improves gene-level correlations of test set genes

Key Impact: These findings exemplify a cross-species transfer learning methodology for sequence-to-function models, and position DanioDecima as a practical resource for zebrafish regulatory engineering

Methodology: Deep Learning techniques were central to the study design.

5. Optical genome mapping identifies source-associated structural variant differences across early-passage human iPSCs

BIORXIV · DOI: 10.64898/2026.05.29.728843 · Score: █████░░░░░ 0.580

Core Problem: ** The reprogramming of somatic cells into induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) can introduce genomic structural variants (SVs), and current quality control methods lack the necessary resolution or sensitivity to comprehensively detect these complex, low-level changes. **

Proposed Solution: ** The authors compared iPSC clones derived from two different somatic sources—peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) and fibroblasts (FBCs)—using Optical Genome Mapping (OGM) to determine if the starting cell type influences the structural variant burden and profile post-reprogramming. **

Key Impact: ** The differential SV profiles observed between PBMC-iPSCs and FBC-iPSCs underscore the critical need for high-resolution, clone-level genomic assessment using techniques like OGM before using iPSCs for downstream disease modeling or research applications. **

Methodology: ** The study utilized OGM, a technique that analyzes fluorescently labeled ultra-high-molecular-weight DNA to detect SVs at a resolution of approximately $\ge 2$ kbp, providing molecule-level evidence of copy-number and balanced rearrangements.
