1. PD-L1-linked spatial decoupling of tumour-immune interactions in EBV-positive DLBCL

MEDRXIV · DOI: 10.64898/2026.07.02.26357123 · Score: ███████░░░ 0.706

Core Problem: EBV-positive diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) is an aggressive lymphoma enriched in elderly patients, in which PD-L1 genomic gains cooperate with viral signalling to promote immune escape

Proposed Solution: However, the spatial mechanisms by which PD-L1 reshapes the tumour microenvironment remain unresolved. Here, we integrate genomic profiling with spatial transcriptomics, spatial proteomics, and single-cell RNA sequencing to resolve the tumour-immune architecture of EBV-positive DLBCL

Key Impact: Together, these findings reveal a PD-L1 driven spatial immune evasion architecture that permits immune infiltration while enforcing multilayered suppression through fibroblast-mediated exclusion, metabolic constraint, and T-cell dysfunction, ultimately preventing effective anti-tumour immunity in EBV-positive DLBCL

Methodology: Single-Cell techniques were central to the study design.

2. Saturation-seq integrates single-cell saturation genome editing and RNA-seq to quantify NFE2L2 (NRF2) variant effects

BIORXIV · DOI: 10.64898/2026.06.30.735631 · Score: ███████░░░ 0.703

Core Problem: Interpreting the functional consequences of variants remains one of the central unsolved problems in genomics and clinical genetics

Proposed Solution: Compounding this, most existing approaches rely on reductive, one-dimensional proxies such as cell growth to score variant effects, which can be a poor substitute for the rich, multidimensional phenotyping that is ultimately needed to understand how variants alter biology. This is especially true for variants known to act through gain-of-function/neomorphic mechanisms

Key Impact: Thus, we establish a broadly applicable high-resolution single-cell variant-to-function platform with a rich phenotypic readout

Methodology: Crispr techniques were central to the study design.

3. Genetic Determinants of Pulmonary Artery Size in over 50,000 Subjects with and without COPD

MEDRXIV · DOI: 10.64898/2026.07.01.26357039 · Score: ██████░░░░ 0.699

Core Problem: Rationale: Pulmonary artery (PA) enlargement is a non-invasive imaging biomarker associated with pulmonary hypertension and mortality in COPD; however, its genetic determinants remain incompletely understood

Proposed Solution: Objectives: To characterize the genetic architecture of PA size across COPD-enriched and population-based cohorts. Methods: We performed genome-wide association analyses of PA diameter using whole-genome sequencing in COPDGene (n=9,418) and ECLIPSE (n=1,859), and imputed-genotype data from the UK Biobank (n=37,073)

Key Impact: The prioritized effector genes bridge rare-variant pulmonary hypertension biology with common-variant systemic vascular biology

Methodology: Genome-Wide techniques were central to the study design.

4. A High-Confidence Atlas of Protein Methylation Enables AI-Driven Detection of Methylated Peptides

BIORXIV · DOI: 10.64898/2026.07.01.733993 · Score: ██████░░░░ 0.674

Core Problem: Lysine and arginine methylation regulate chromatin dynamics, transcription, and cellular signaling, however confident mass spectrometry (MS)-based detection and localization of this modification remain challenging

Proposed Solution: We reanalyzed eight public human methylation-enriched datasets using an open and standardized workflow that integrates database searching via the Trans-Proteomic Pipeline with a decoy-based statistical method for the independent estimation of false localization rates (FLR). This yielded a high-confidence Human Methylation Atlas of 1,828 sites (57 methyl-lysine, 1,771 methyl-arginine) across 1,021 proteins, classified into Gold, Silver, and Bronze confidence tiers

Key Impact: The atlas is available through PTMeXchange and PRIDE, with curated site evidence integrated into UniProt and PeptideAtlas

Methodology: Deep Learning techniques were central to the study design.

5. CryoROLE: describing large inter-domain rotation in single particle cryo-EM

BIORXIV · DOI: 10.64898/2026.07.04.736454 · Score: ██████░░░░ 0.663

Core Problem: In single particle cryo-EM, analysis of continuous conformational heterogeneity has always been challenging

Proposed Solution: Both linear and deep learning-based methods treat conformational heterogeneity as perturbations to the consensus average conformation, limiting their capability in analyzing large protein motions. While classic conformational classifications are capable of handling large domain motion, they bin continuous protein dynamics into discrete static substates

Key Impact: Applying it to various biological systems reveals hidden conformational dynamics that are relevant to protein functions

Methodology: Deep Learning techniques were central to the study design.
