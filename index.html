2. Gigabase-scale deletion scanning of the human genome

BIORXIV · DOI: 10.64898/2026.05.29.728882 · Score: ███████░░░ 0.743

Core Problem: ** The essentiality of human genomic content remains largely unknown because current mapping efforts have focused almost exclusively on individual protein-coding open reading frames (ORFs), neglecting noncoding and architectural regions. **

Proposed Solution: ** The authors developed Shred-seq, a platform that couples Type I-C CRISPR-Cas3 deletions with phage polymerase-based genotyping to perform large-scale deletion scans across the human genome. This method generates thousands of independent deletions ranging from hundreds of base pairs up to hundreds of kilobases. **

Key Impact: ** Shred-seq provides an empirical means to systematically quantify purifying selection and delineate the genomic content—both coding and noncoding—required for human cellular viability, potentially paving the way toward defining a minimal human genome. **

Methodology: ** The technique captures deletion breakpoints at high resolution by utilizing flanking phage polymerase promoters for in vitro or in situ transcription, allowing simultaneous bulk or single-cell phenotyping of the deleted regions.

3. Deciphering context-dependent epigenetic program by network-based prediction of clustered open regulatory elements from single-cell chromatin accessibility

BIORXIV · DOI: 10.64898/2026.03.17.712366 · Score: ███████░░░ 0.727

Core Problem: Large cis-regulatory domains, spanning tens to hundreds of kilobases, are pivotal in orchestrating cell-state-specific transcriptional programs that define cellular identity

Proposed Solution: However, existing single-cell analytical frameworks lack the capacity to identify these higher-order structures, thereby obscuring the coordinated, domain-level epigenetic regulation essential for complex biological processes. To address this, we introduce enCORE, a computational framework that leverages enhancer-enhancer interaction networks to determine Clustered Open Regulatory Elements (COREs) solely from single-cell ATAC-sequencing data

Key Impact: Collectively, our framework provides a powerful and scalable platform for deciphering the complex epigenetic architectures underlying human development and disease

Methodology: Single-Cell techniques were central to the study design.

5. Combining transcriptomic resolutions and machine learning strategies uncovers new OXPHOS genes in Caenorhabditis elegans

Core Problem: Assigning functions to genes remains a major challenge in biology, as a large fraction of genes remain unannotated despite the availability of complete genomes

Proposed Solution: Oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS), the primary source of ATP in eukaryotes, exemplifies this gap: although it has been extensively studied in mammals, our understanding of this process in other lineages remains limited. In general, research in other organisms has relied on the identification of sequence homologs of genes previously characterized in mammals

Key Impact: Our results highlight the value of integrating biological priors, complementary learning paradigms, and multi-resolution transcriptomic data to enable systematic gene function discovery
